Data and services

  • Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License (ODbL 1.0), served by OpenFreeMap.
  • Routing, map matching and posted limits: Valhalla (MIT) on the FOSSGIS public server.
  • Address search: Nominatim (OpenStreetMap data, ODbL). Search suggestions: Photon by Komoot (OpenStreetMap data, ODbL).
  • Road network for routing (data/graph): derived from OpenStreetMap (Geofabrik extracts) © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0; the derived road tiles are offered under the same licence.
  • Elevation: AWS Terrain Tiles (Terrarium), from the sources listed at registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles.
  • Satellite imagery (developer accounts only): USGS The National Map, Orthoimagery (USDA NAIP and USGS), public domain.
  • Plate-reading cameras: OpenStreetMap (surveillance:type=ALPR, DeFlock), ODbL.
  • Route ranking: land use, buildings, businesses, schools, streams, signals, stop signs, speed limits, lanes, warning signs, guard rails, cycle routes, scenic byways and fuel stations: OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0, via OpenFreeMap tiles and the public Overpass servers (overpass-api.de, Kumi Systems, private.coffee, VK Maps).
  • Fuel stations, road lighting, tunnels and cycle routes (route planning): OpenStreetMap (amenity=fuel, lit, tunnel, route=bicycle), ODbL.
  • EV chargers: the Alternative Fuel Stations data of the US Department of Energy (National Laboratory of the Rockies, formerly NREL), public domain.
  • Weather: Weather data by Open-Meteo.com, CC BY 4.0. Current conditions: US National Weather Service station observations (api.weather.gov), public domain. Sun position: NOAA's solar calculation, worked out on the phone.
  • Rivers: NOAA National Water Prediction Service flood categories and U.S. Geological Survey real-time stream gauges (public domain).
  • Work zones and closures: state Work Zone Data Exchange feeds from the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet, Indiana DOT and, with its key, Ohio DOT (OHGO); public data, provided as is. Closures, roadwork, slow traffic and crashes on the map: Indiana DOT's CARS traveler information (public domain), the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet's WZDx feed and, with its key, Ohio DOT's OHGO incidents, construction, travel delays, dangerous slowdowns, road weather sensors and message signs; provided as is. Road cameras: Ohio DOT's traffic camera pictures (OHGO) and the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet's camera list with TRIMARC's pictures, shown as published.
  • Harvest: USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service Crop Progress (public domain) and USDA Agricultural Handbook 628. Park seasons: National Park Service Visitor Use Statistics (public domain).
  • Deer seasons: Ohio State Highway Patrol, Kentucky Transportation Cabinet, Indiana State Police, West Virginia DNR, Tennessee DOT and Virginia DMV crash figures.
  • Games: MLB schedules, Copyright MLB Advanced Media, L.P. (gdx.mlb.com/components/copyright.txt). Events: Event data by SeatGeek, when set up.
  • Photo locations: Wikimedia Commons (positions only).
  • Traffic signs: © Mapillary, CC BY-SA 4.0 (when set up).
  • Place photos: © the photographer named under each, via Mapillary, CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • Route context (preprocessed into the app's context grid; US Government works are public domain): night lights from NASA's Black Marble annual night-time lights (VNP46A4, VIIRS Day/Night Band; Román et al. 2018, doi:10.5067/VIIRS/VNP46A4.002) from the NASA LAADS DAAC, or where not yet rebuilt the Black Marble 2016 image, courtesy of NASA Earth Observatory, served by NASA GIBS; impervious surface (NLCD 2021) and tree canopy (NLCD/USFS Tree Canopy Cover 2021) from USGS and the MRLC consortium; road noise from the US DOT Bureau of Transportation Statistics National Transportation Noise Map; housing units and residents (2020 Census blocks) and median year built (American Community Survey 5-year) from the US Census Bureau; address points from OpenAddresses contributors and the county and state agencies that publish them (each under its own terms; only counts per area are kept); traffic volume, pavement roughness, speed limits, lanes and functional class from the FHWA Highway Performance Monitoring System and the state DOTs; fatal crashes from the NHTSA Fatality Analysis Reporting System; flood hazard zones from the FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer; crop types from the USDA NASS Cropland Data Layer (via Microsoft Planetary Computer); park boundaries and visitor counts from the National Park Service. None of these agencies endorses Vetta.

Website only

The website can speak road names with transformers.js (Apache License 2.0, bundling onnxruntime-web, MIT), downloaded when that switch is turned on. The iOS app never loads it.
